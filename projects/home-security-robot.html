Chief Design Engineer

UNIVERSITY OF ARIZONA
01 · OVERVIEW

Designing a Mobile Security System

This project challenged a four-person engineering team to design and build a functional home security robot. The system was designed around a mobile platform capable of navigating its environment while using sensors and a camera system to monitor its surroundings.

As the Chief Design Engineer, I led the mechanical design of the robot and coordinated the mechanical work across the team. I was responsible for turning the system requirements into a physical design that could actually be fabricated, assembled, and tested.

The project gave me the opportunity to take a mechanical system from concept through CAD, analysis, component selection, fabrication, assembly, and demonstration.

02 · FEATURED DESIGN

Robot Architecture

A compact mobile platform with an integrated camera turret

The mechanical system was built around a mobile drive platform with an elevated camera system. The design had to package the drive components, electronics, sensors, and camera while maintaining enough stability for the robot to move and operate reliably.

Mobile Drive

Developed the mechanical drive system and used a pitman-style drive mechanism to convert motor rotation into controlled wheel motion.

Camera Turret

Designed an elevated rotating camera assembly to expand the robot's field of view while integrating with the main chassis.

Structural Design

Developed the chassis and supporting components in SolidWorks with consideration for strength, weight, manufacturability, and component packaging.

System Integration

Integrated mechanical components with motors, sensors, electronics, and the Arduino-based control system.

05 · CONTROLS & INTEGRATION

Mechanical Meets Electrical

The robot required the mechanical system to work closely with its electronics and controls. Mechanical packaging had to account for motors, sensors, wiring, the control board, and the camera system while leaving enough access for assembly and troubleshooting.

An Arduino-based Lafvin UNO R3 platform was used for the robot's control system, including an ultrasonic sensor demonstration. The project provided hands-on experience integrating physical hardware with embedded controls rather than treating the mechanical and electrical systems as separate designs.

Arduino

Used an Arduino-based control platform to interface with sensors and control system functions.

Ultrasonic Sensing

Integrated ultrasonic sensing for environmental detection and obstacle awareness.

Camera System

Integrated the camera into the mechanical turret to provide a mobile observation platform.

Packaging

Designed around the physical constraints created by electronics, motors, sensors, and moving components.

06 · ENGINEERING CHALLENGE

Building Around Real Constraints

One of the biggest challenges was balancing the competing requirements of a mobile robot. The structure needed to be strong enough to support the camera system and survive movement, while remaining compact enough for the drive system and electronics.

Every design decision affected another part of the system. Component placement influenced the center of mass, the chassis affected packaging, and the drive geometry affected how the robot moved. This forced the mechanical design to be developed as part of the complete system rather than as isolated components.

07 · TAKEAWAYS

What I Built

This project was one of my first opportunities to lead a complete mechanical system while working directly with a small engineering team. I was responsible for more than just individual CAD parts. I had to think about how the parts moved, how they were manufactured, how they fit together, and how they interacted with the electrical system.

The project strengthened my interest in mechanical design work where CAD, analysis, fabrication, and hands-on problem solving all come together in a physical product.
