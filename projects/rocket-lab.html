01 / OVERVIEW

Characterizing Optical Hardware

For senior design, my team is developing a modular focal plane characterization platform for Rocket Lab Optical Systems. The system is intended to provide a flexible alternative to commercial characterization stations that can cost tens to hundreds of thousands of dollars.

The platform will allow focal plane assemblies to be evaluated across multiple optical and electrical performance characteristics, including modulation transfer function, quantum efficiency, linearity, gain states, and noise.

My role is focused on the mechanical design and integration of the system, while also leading team integration and project safety. This includes developing the mechanical architecture, evaluating requirements and design trades, building prototypes, and coordinating the interfaces between mechanical, optical, and electrical subsystems.

02 / HIGHLIGHTED PROJECTS

Engineering Projects

Modular Focal Plane Platform

The system combines precision motion, optical hardware, electronics, software, and data acquisition into a single characterization platform. The mechanical architecture must maintain alignment and stability while allowing the system to be assembled, adjusted, tested, and used in a cleanroom environment.

The mechanical system is designed around approximately 0.5 µm translation requirements and sub-pixel positioning stability. It integrates the focal plane with the optical components, illumination hardware, and characterization path, and is designed for an operating temperature range of approximately -10°C to 50°C.

~0.5 µm TRANSLATION · -10°C TO 50°C OPERATING RANGE

Design Constraints & Requirements

The design process begins with translating the sponsor's characterization requirements into mechanical requirements that can be designed, fabricated, and tested.

Key requirements include approximately 0.5 µm translation with sufficient stability for sub-pixel optical characterization, and operation across approximately -10°C to 50°C while maintaining mechanical and optical alignment. The system must also be compatible with cleanroom operation, materials, and assembly practices.

Components have to be fabricated and assembled using available machining and prototyping capabilities, with defined mechanical interfaces between the optics, electronics, motion components, and the focal plane.

Mechanical Design & Integration

I'm developing the mechanical layout and interfaces required to position the focal plane, optical components, actuators, and supporting hardware within a compact test platform. I'm creating the system and its components in SolidWorks, developing assemblies, evaluating component interfaces, and preparing the design for fabrication.

Design decisions are made by weighing requirements, cost, schedule, manufacturability, material selection, and system integration. I'm building early prototypes using 3D printing and planning final components around available machining capabilities, including manual milling, turning, drilling, and sawing.

I'm also developing manufacturing drawings and applying dimensioning and GD&T practices to communicate design requirements to fabrication, and coordinating mechanical interfaces with the optical, electrical, and software portions of the project so the complete system works toward the same requirements.

ROLES: MECHANICAL ENGINEER · INTEGRATION LEAD · SAFETY LEAD

Analysis & Validation

As the mechanical design develops, the next stage is to validate the structure and thermal behavior of the platform through engineering analysis and physical testing.

Planned work includes ANSYS thermal simulations, refinement of the mechanical design, manufacturing drawings, hardware fabrication, and subsystem integration. The final system will be evaluated against the original positioning, environmental, optical, and integration requirements before delivery to the sponsor.
