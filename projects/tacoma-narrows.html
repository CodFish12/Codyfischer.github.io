UNIVERSITY OF ARIZONA
01 · OVERVIEW

Modeling a Bridge Collapse

This project used MATLAB to model the aeroelastic flutter behavior of the Tacoma Narrows Bridge and investigate the wind conditions associated with its eventual collapse.

The goal was to take a real engineering failure and build a mathematical model capable of predicting the onset of unstable aerodynamic behavior. The simulation was then compared against historical conditions from the actual bridge collapse.

The project combined mechanical engineering fundamentals, differential equations, numerical methods, and computational modeling to connect a mathematical model to a real-world structural failure.

02 · FEATURED ANALYSIS

Predicting when aerodynamic forces become unstable

The MATLAB simulation modeled the interaction between the bridge structure and aerodynamic loading as wind speed increased. The model was used to identify the critical wind speed at which the bridge's response transitioned toward unstable flutter.

04 · MODEL DEVELOPMENT

Building the Simulation

The simulation was developed by translating the physical behavior of the bridge into a computational model. MATLAB provided a way to repeatedly solve the system while varying the wind conditions and observing how the structural response changed.

Rather than treating the historical collapse as a purely qualitative case study, the project used engineering analysis to investigate why increasing aerodynamic excitation could drive the structure into an unstable response.

Input Parameters

Defined the structural and aerodynamic parameters required to represent the bridge's behavior.

Governing Equations

Converted the physical system into mathematical equations that could be solved computationally.

Wind-Speed Sweep

Evaluated the system across changing wind speeds to identify the transition toward unstable behavior.

Response Analysis

Examined the simulated structural response to determine the critical condition predicted by the model.

05 · RESULT

Simulation vs. Reality

MODEL ACCURACY

Within 5%

The MATLAB model predicted a critical wind speed within approximately 5% of the documented real-world conditions associated with the Tacoma Narrows Bridge collapse.

The result provided a useful validation of the model while also demonstrating the limitations of representing a complex aeroelastic phenomenon with a simplified engineering model.

06 · ENGINEERING TAKEAWAY

Why It Matters

The most valuable part of this project was connecting mathematical analysis to a physical engineering failure. The simulation showed how structural dynamics and aerodynamic forces can interact to produce behavior that would not be obvious from looking at the structure alone.

It also reinforced the value of engineering models as tools for understanding complex systems. A model does not need to reproduce every physical detail to provide useful insight, but its assumptions and limitations have to be understood when interpreting the results.
